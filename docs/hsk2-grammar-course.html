F01 · Stress in two-syllable words

Pattern: medium + STRONG (most words); STRONG + light (neutral-tone words)

Most two-syllable words carry a little more weight on the second syllable: 旅游, 运动, 机场. Words whose second syllable is in the neutral tone put the weight on the first syllable and keep the second one short and light: 眼睛, 意思, 早上. Listening for this rhythm makes speech sound natural even before every tone is perfect.

运动

yùn dòng

sport; to exercise (second syllable slightly stronger)

眼睛

yǎn jing

eye (first syllable strong, second light)

Common mistake: Do not give every syllable the same full length and loudness. Neutral-tone syllables are shorter and lighter, not just “toneless.”

Practice this point

  1. Which syllable is stronger in 旅游?

  2. Which syllable is light in 意思?

Show model answers and explanations

  1. The second syllable, 游.

    Ordinary two-syllable words without a neutral tone lean slightly toward the second syllable.

  2. The second syllable, si.

    It is a neutral-tone syllable, so it is short and light.

G01 · Say what you want to do with 要

Pattern: subject + 要 + action; negative: 不想 + action

要 before a verb expresses a firm wish or plan: the speaker wants to, or intends to, do something. It sounds more decided than 想, which you met in HSK 1. When you say you do not want to do something, the usual negative is 不想, because 不要 before a verb normally means “do not” as an instruction (module 10).

周末我要去游泳。

Zhōu mò wǒ yào qù yóu yǒng.

I want to go swimming this weekend.

你要喝咖啡还是茶？

Nǐ yào hē kā fēi hái shi chá?

Would you like coffee or tea?

Common mistake: Do not answer “I don’t want to” with 我不要去 when you mean a plain lack of wish; 我不想去 is the neutral negative. 不要 + verb sounds like telling someone not to do it.

Practice this point

  1. Say “I want to travel to Beijing.”

  2. Answer “你要喝茶吗？” negatively, meaning you do not feel like it.

Show model answers and explanations

  1. 我要去北京旅游。

    要 comes directly before the intended action; no word for “to” is needed.

  2. 我不想喝。

    Use 不想 for “I don’t want to”; 不要喝 would sound like an instruction.

G02 · Mark the top of a scale with 最

Pattern: subject + 最 + adjective / feeling verb

最 means “most” and goes directly before an adjective or a verb of feeling such as 喜欢. It marks the highest degree within a group, which may be stated or understood. No extra word is needed after the adjective, and 最 is not used together with 很 on the same word.

我最喜欢踢足球。

Wǒ zuì xǐ huan tī zú qiú.

Football is my favorite.

九月的天气最好。

Jiǔ yuè de tiān qì zuì hǎo.

The weather is best in September.

Common mistake: Avoid 最很好 or 很最好. Choose one degree word: 最 for “the most,” 很 for an ordinary description.

Practice this point

  1. Say “My younger brother is the tallest.”

  2. Ask “Which sport do you like most?”

Show model answers and explanations

  1. 我弟弟最高。

    最 goes before the adjective 高; 是 is not needed.

  2. 你最喜欢什么运动？

    最 modifies 喜欢 and 什么 stays where the answer would go.

G03 · Give rough numbers with 几 and 多

Pattern: 十/二十… + 几 + measure word; whole number + 多 + measure word

Besides asking a question, 几 can stand for an unknown small digit inside a statement: 十几个 is “a dozen or so” (11–19), 几十个 is “several dozen.” 多 after a round number means “more than”: 二十多个 is “twenty-odd.” Put 多 after the round number and before the measure word for whole units.

教室里有十几个学生。

Jiào shì li yǒu shí jǐ ge xué sheng.

There are a dozen or so students in the classroom.

这本书三十多块钱。

Zhè běn shū sān shí duō kuài qián.

This book costs thirty-something yuan.

Common mistake: Do not put 多 after the measure word for whole numbers: say 二十多个人, not 二十个多人. A statement with 几 has no question mark.

Practice this point

  1. Say “more than forty people”.

  2. What does 我们班有十几个同学 mean?

Show model answers and explanations

  1. 四十多个人

    多 follows the round number and comes before the measure word.

  2. There are somewhere between eleven and nineteen classmates in our class.

    十几 means ten plus an unspecified small number.

F02 · Stress in three-syllable words

Pattern: medium + light + STRONG; medium + STRONG + light; STRONG + light + light

Three-syllable words have a small number of common rhythms. Most end on the strongest syllable, as in 火车站 and 服务员. Some have a light final syllable, as in 为什么, and a few have a strong first syllable followed by two light ones, as in 朋友们. Treat these as listening habits rather than rules to memorize.

火车站

huǒ chē zhàn

railway station (last syllable strongest)

为什么

wèi shén me

why (final syllable light)

Common mistake: Do not stress a neutral-tone syllable. In 为什么, the final me is light.

Practice this point

  1. Is the last syllable of 为什么 strong or light?

  2. Say 火车站 with the weight on the correct syllable.

Show model answers and explanations

  1. Light.

    It carries the neutral tone.

  2. huǒ chē ZHÀN

    The final syllable carries the most weight in this word.

G04 · Check a guess with 是不是

Pattern: statement + 是不是？ / subject + 是不是 + predicate？

是不是 asks the listener to confirm something you already suspect. It can go at the end of a complete statement or just before the predicate. Unlike 吗, it signals that you expect the answer to be yes. Answer with 是 / 是的 or 不是 and, ideally, a full sentence.

你生病了，是不是？

Nǐ shēng bìng le, shì bu shì?

You’re ill, aren’t you?

你是不是每天跑步？

Nǐ shì bu shì měi tiān pǎo bù?

You run every day, right?

Common mistake: Do not add 吗 to a 是不是 question; it is already a question. Also avoid splitting it as 是…不是 around the predicate in this pattern.

Practice this point

  1. Turn 他今天很忙 into a 是不是 question at the end.

  2. Ask “Did you get up at six?” using 是不是 before the predicate.

Show model answers and explanations

  1. 他今天很忙，是不是？

    Put a comma after the statement and add 是不是.

  2. 你是不是六点起床的？ / 你是不是六点起床？

    是不是 sits between the subject and what you want confirmed.

G05 · Say “every” with 每

Pattern: 每 + (number) + measure word + noun (+ 都)

每 means “every” or “each.” Like 这 and 那, it needs a measure word before an ordinary noun: 每个人, 每本书. Time words such as 天 and 年 act as their own measure words, so 每天 and 每年 need nothing extra. 都 often follows in the predicate to stress that there are no exceptions.

我每天早上七点起床。

Wǒ měi tiān zǎo shang qī diǎn qǐ chuáng.

I get up at seven every morning.

每个学生都有一本书。

Měi ge xué sheng dōu yǒu yì běn shū.

Every student has a book.

Common mistake: Do not drop the measure word: 每个人, not 每人 in ordinary beginner speech (每人 exists in set phrases). Do not say 每天天.

Practice this point

  1. Say “He drinks a cup of coffee every day.”

  2. Say “Everyone is busy.” with 每 and 都.

Show model answers and explanations

  1. 他每天喝一杯咖啡。

    天 needs no measure word after 每.

  2. 每个人都很忙。

    Use 个 after 每 and 都 before the predicate.

G06 · Ask how tall, long, or old with 多

Pattern: 多 + one-syllable adjective？ (多高, 多长, 多大, 多远)

多 before an adjective asks “how” + that quality: 多高 “how tall,” 多长 “how long,” 多大 “how old/how big.” The adjective is the positive end of the scale, just as English asks “how tall” rather than “how short.” 有 can come before 多 in the question, as in 你有多高.

你哥哥多高？

Nǐ gē ge duō gāo?

How tall is your older brother?

这条路有多长？

Zhè tiáo lù yǒu duō cháng?

How long is this road?

Common mistake: Do not confuse question 多 + adjective with 多少 for numbers of things. Do not ask 多矮 for height in a neutral question.

Practice this point

  1. Ask “How old is your daughter?”

  2. Answer “他多高？” with “one meter eighty.”

Show model answers and explanations

  1. 你女儿多大？ / 你女儿多大了？

    多大 asks about age; 了 may highlight current age.

  2. 他一米八。

    A height phrase can act as the predicate, like an age.

F03 · Stress in four-character phrases

Pattern: medium + light + medium + STRONG (no neutral tone)

In four-syllable expressions without neutral tones, the last syllable is usually the strongest and the second is a little lighter, giving a balanced rhythm. When one syllable has a neutral tone, that syllable is short and the rest keep their full tones. Practice four-syllable chunks as one unit rather than four separate words.

公共汽车

gōng gòng qì chē

bus (final syllable carries the weight)

一个西瓜

yí ge xī guā

one watermelon (ge is light)

Common mistake: Do not pause between every syllable of 公共汽车; say it as one rhythmic unit.

Practice this point

  1. Which syllable is light in 一个西瓜?

  2. Say 公共汽车 as one unit.

Show model answers and explanations

  1. ge (个)

    The measure word 个 is normally neutral here.

  2. gōng gòng qì CHĒ

    Keep it smooth with the final syllable strongest.

G07 · Refer to a thing with a 的 phrase

Pattern: adjective / noun / pronoun + 的 (noun understood)

When the noun is already clear from context, Mandarin can leave it out and keep only the description with 的: 红的 “the red one,” 我的 “mine,” 新的 “the new one.” The 的 phrase then acts like a noun and can be a subject or an object.

左边那个红的是我的。

Zuǒ bian nà ge hóng de shì wǒ de.

The red one on the left is mine.

我不要旧的，我要新的。

Wǒ bú yào jiù de, wǒ yào xīn de.

I don’t want the old one; I want the new one.

Common mistake: Do not drop 的: 我要红 does not mean “I want the red one.” Also make sure the omitted noun is clear to the listener.

Practice this point

  1. Say “This watch is my husband’s.”

  2. Ask “Which one do you want, the big one or the small one?”

Show model answers and explanations

  1. 这块手表是我丈夫的。 / 这个手表是我丈夫的。

    The 的 phrase 我丈夫的 stands for “my husband’s (watch).”

  2. 你要大的还是小的？

    大的 and 小的 refer to the item without repeating the noun.

G08 · Make an action brief or casual with 一下

Pattern: verb + 一下 (+ object)

一下 after a verb makes the action short, light, or tentative: 看一下 “take a look,” 等一下 “wait a moment.” It often makes requests sound softer and friendlier. Put the object after 一下.

请等一下。

Qǐng děng yí xià.

Please wait a moment.

你看一下这本书。

Nǐ kàn yí xià zhè běn shū.

Have a look at this book.

Common mistake: Do not put 一下 before the verb or after the object: 介绍一下你的朋友, not 介绍你的朋友一下 in this beginner pattern.

Practice this point

  1. Politely say “Let me introduce (someone) for a moment.”

  2. Say “Please ask the teacher.” softly.

Show model answers and explanations

  1. 我介绍一下。

    一下 softens the action of introducing.

  2. 请问一下老师。 / 你问一下老师。

    Place 一下 directly after 问 and the object after it.

G09 · Add feeling with 真

Pattern: 真 + adjective (exclamation)

真 means “really” and expresses the speaker’s genuine feeling about something in front of them: 真好 “that’s great,” 真漂亮 “so pretty.” It turns a description into an exclamation, so it is not normally used in a neutral comparison or after 很.

这件衣服真漂亮！

Zhè jiàn yī fu zhēn piào liang!

This piece of clothing is really pretty!

你的房间真大！

Nǐ de fáng jiān zhēn dà!

Your room is really big!

Common mistake: Do not use 真 before a noun phrase as a modifier: 真漂亮的衣服 is unnatural at this stage; say 这件衣服真漂亮. Do not combine 真很.

Practice this point

  1. React to delicious food with 真.

  2. Fix the error: 今天真很冷。

Show model answers and explanations

  1. 真好吃！ / 这个菜真好吃！

    真 goes directly before the adjective 好吃.

  2. 今天真冷。

    Use only one degree word before 冷.

G10 · Highlight the person who did something with 是…的

Pattern: subject + 是 + person + verb + 的

In HSK 1 you used 是…的 to highlight the time, place, or manner of a known past event. The same frame can spotlight the person responsible: the event is already known, and the new information is who did it. Put 是 before the doer and 的 after the verb.

这个工作是我朋友帮我介绍的。

Zhè ge gōng zuò shì wǒ péng you bāng wǒ jiè shào de.

It was my friend who recommended this job to me.

这个蛋糕是妈妈做的。

Zhè ge dàn gāo shì mā ma zuò de.

It was Mom who made this cake.

Common mistake: Do not use 是…的 for a new event the listener does not know about yet; use 了 to report it. Do not drop 的.

Practice this point

  1. Say “It was the teacher who told me.”

  2. Ask “Who bought this watch?” emphasizing the buyer.

Show model answers and explanations

  1. 是老师告诉我的。

    是 marks the doer; 的 closes the frame.

  2. 这块手表是谁买的？ / 这个手表是谁买的？

    谁 sits where the doer goes in the 是…的 frame.

G11 · Say when with ……的时候

Pattern: event + 的时候, main clause

……的时候 means “when …” or “at the time of …”. Put the whole event before 的时候, then give the main sentence. Unlike English, the “when” part always comes first.

我生日的时候，朋友送了我一本书。

Wǒ shēng rì de shí hou, péng you sòng le wǒ yì běn shū.

On my birthday, a friend gave me a book.

吃饭的时候别看手机。

Chī fàn de shí hou bié kàn shǒu jī.

Don’t look at your phone while eating.

Common mistake: Do not put the 的时候 clause at the end as in English “…when I was ill.” Do not drop 的: 吃饭时候 is incomplete in speech.

Practice this point

  1. Say “When I was little, I liked cats.”

  2. Combine: 我到家 / 妈妈在做饭.

Show model answers and explanations

  1. 我小的时候很喜欢猫。 / 我小的时候喜欢猫。

    The time clause comes first.

  2. 我到家的时候，妈妈在做饭。

    的时候 attaches to the first event.

G12 · Report that something has already happened with 已经

Pattern: subject + 已经 + verb / state + 了

已经 means “already.” It goes before the verb or adjective, and the sentence usually ends with 了 to show the situation has now changed. It suits times, ages, and completed actions.

电影已经开始了。

Diàn yǐng yǐ jīng kāi shǐ le.

The movie has already started.

他已经二十岁了。

Tā yǐ jīng èr shí suì le.

He’s already twenty.

Common mistake: Do not put 已经 at the end of the sentence, and do not forget the final 了 in these examples.

Practice this point

  1. Say “It’s already ten o’clock.”

  2. Say “I have already eaten.”

Show model answers and explanations

  1. 已经十点了。 / 现在已经十点了。

    已经 + time + 了 signals a reached point.

  2. 我已经吃饭了。 / 我已经吃了。

    已经 precedes the verb; 了 marks the change.

F04 · Sentence stress on modifiers

Pattern: stress the describing word in 的 phrases and before verbs

In connected speech the word that carries new information is said a little more strongly. A modifier before a noun or a time/place phrase before a verb often carries this weight: 红色的衣服, 早上跑步. This helps a listener catch exactly which item or when.

我最喜欢红色的衣服。

Wǒ zuì xǐ huan HÓNG SÈ de yī fu.

I like red clothes best. (stress 红色)

我早上跑步。

Wǒ ZǍO SHANG pǎo bù.

I run in the morning. (stress 早上)

Common mistake: Do not stress 的 itself; it is light.

Practice this point

  1. In 那是我妈妈做的菜, which word is stressed when answering “Who made it?”

  2. Is 的 stressed in 红色的衣服?

Show model answers and explanations

  1. 妈妈

    The new information is the person.

  2. No, 的 is light.

    Structural particles are unstressed.

G13 · Draw a conclusion with 就

Pattern: (reason / situation), 就 + verb

After the situation has been laid out, 就 introduces the decision or natural result: “then,” “in that case,” or “just.” It goes before the verb, often with 吧 to make a suggestion.

这件不贵，就买这件吧。

Zhè jiàn bú guì, jiù mǎi zhè jiàn ba.

This one isn’t expensive, so let’s just buy this one.

你累了，就在家休息吧。

Nǐ lèi le, jiù zài jiā xiū xi ba.

You’re tired, so just rest at home.

Common mistake: Do not put 就 before the subject: 我就去, not 就我去 in this meaning.

Practice this point

  1. Your friend doesn’t want to go out. Suggest eating at home with 就.

  2. Say “If you like it, just buy it.”

Show model answers and explanations

  1. 那就在家吃吧。 / 我们就在家吃吧。

    就 introduces the resulting suggestion.

  2. 你喜欢就买吧。

    就 links the condition to the decision.

G14 · Say “passable, not bad” with 还

Pattern: 还 + 好 / 可以 / 不错

还 before a positive adjective softens it to “fairly,” “okay,” “not bad.” 还好 and 还可以 are common, modest answers to “How is it?” They sound polite and are weaker than 很好.

A: 考试怎么样？ B: 还可以。

A: Kǎo shì zěn me yàng? B: Hái kě yǐ.

A: How was the exam? B: It was okay.

这家店的咖啡还不错。

Zhè jiā diàn de kā fēi hái bú cuò.

The coffee at this shop is not bad.

Common mistake: Do not read this 还 as “still” or “also”; before 好, 可以, or 不错 it means “passably.”

Practice this point

  1. Give a modest answer to 你身体怎么样？

  2. Say “This dish is pretty good.” modestly.

Show model answers and explanations

  1. 还好。 / 还可以。

    还 softens the positive adjective.

  2. 这个菜还不错。

    还不错 means “not bad, fairly good.”

G15 · Complain gently with 有点儿

Pattern: 有点儿 + adjective / feeling verb (usually negative)

有点儿 means “a bit” and goes before an adjective or verb of feeling. It usually signals that something is not quite as the speaker would like: 有点儿贵 “a bit pricey,” 有点儿累 “a little tired.” For a neutral or positive small amount after an adjective, use 一点儿 instead (module 12).

这件衣服有点儿大。

Zhè jiàn yī fu yǒu diǎnr dà.

This piece of clothing is a bit big.

今天我有点儿累。

Jīn tiān wǒ yǒu diǎnr lèi.

I’m a little tired today.

Common mistake: Avoid 有点儿好 for praise; it sounds odd. Do not put 有点儿 after the adjective: 贵一点儿 is a comparison, not a complaint.

Practice this point

  1. Complain that the coffee is a bit expensive.

  2. Choose the better sentence for “a bit cold (and I don’t like it)”: 有点儿冷 or 冷一点儿?

Show model answers and explanations

  1. 这杯咖啡有点儿贵。

    有点儿 goes before the adjective.

  2. 有点儿冷

    有点儿 + adjective expresses mild dissatisfaction.

G16 · Ask “how come?” with 怎么

Pattern: subject + 怎么 + (不/没) + verb？

In HSK 1, 怎么 asked about method. Placed before a verb in a question about a surprising situation, it asks the reason with a note of surprise: “How come…?” It is often used with 不 or 没 for something that is not happening as expected.

你怎么不吃了？

Nǐ zěn me bù chī le?

他怎么还没来？

Tā zěn me hái méi lái?

How come he hasn’t arrived yet?

G17 · Say “every single one” by doubling a measure word

这些衣服件件都很漂亮。

Zhè xiē yī fu jiàn jiàn dōu hěn piào liang.

他天天都去跑步。

Tā tiān tiān dōu qù pǎo bù.

He goes running every day.

Common mistake: Do not put the doubled measure word after the verb as an object, and do not add a number: 一个个 has a different meaning (“one by one”).

  2. Say “I drink milk every day.” with a reduplicated word.

    个个 before 都 means “every single one.”

  2. 我天天喝牛奶。 / 我天天都喝牛奶。

    天天 means “every day.”

G18 · Give cause and effect with 因为…所以

Pattern: 因为 + reason，所以 + result

因为 introduces the reason and 所以 introduces the result. Either word can be used alone, but using both makes the logic very clear. The reason normally comes first.

因为下雨，所以我们没去踢足球。

Yīn wèi xià yǔ, suǒ yǐ wǒ men méi qù tī zú qiú.

Because it rained, we didn’t go to play football.

Yīn wèi tā shēng bìng le, suǒ yǐ jīn tiān méi lái.

Because he is ill, he didn’t come today.

Common mistake: Do not put 所以 before the reason. In English “so” can join loosely; in Chinese keep 所以 at the start of the result clause.

  2. Answer “你为什么学汉语？” using 因为.

    Reason first, result second.

  2. 因为我想去中国旅游。 (any reasonable reason)

    因为 introduces the reason in an answer.

F05 · Rising and falling sentence intonation

Pattern: statements fall; questions without question words often rise

Tones belong to syllables, but a whole sentence also has a melody. Statements usually fall at the end. A short question with no 吗 or question word can rise to show it is a question. The individual tones keep their shapes; the overall pitch shifts.

他姓张。

Tā xìng Zhāng. ↘

His surname is Zhang. (falling)

他姓张？

Tā xìng Zhāng? ↗

His surname is Zhang? (rising)

Common mistake: Do not flatten or change the syllable tones to make a question; raise the overall pitch instead.

Practice this point

  1. Does 你不喜欢喝咖啡。 rise or fall?

  2. How do you turn 你是老师 into a question without 吗?

Show model answers and explanations

  1. It falls.

    It is a statement.

  2. Say it with rising intonation: 你是老师？

    Rising pitch signals the question.

G19 · Say something continues with 还

Pattern: subject + 还 + verb / state; negative: 还没 (有) + verb

This 还 means “still”: an action or state has not ended. Its negative 还没 (有) means “not yet,” often with 呢 at the end. This differs from module 5, where 还 before 好 or 可以 meant “fairly.”

十点了，他还在睡觉。

Shí diǎn le, tā hái zài shuì jiào.

It’s ten o’clock and he is still sleeping.

我还没吃早饭呢。

Wǒ hái méi chī zǎo fàn ne.

I haven’t had breakfast yet.

Common mistake: Do not use 还不 for “not yet” about completed actions; use 还没.

G20 · Show “early” or “quick” with 就

Pattern: time / short duration + 就 + verb + 了

When 就 follows a time word or a short duration, it shows the speaker thinks the event happened early, quickly, or easily. The sentence often ends with 了.

他七点就到学校了。

Tā qī diǎn jiù dào xué xiào le.

He got to school as early as seven.

坐飞机两个小时就到了。

Zuò fēi jī liǎng ge xiǎo shí jiù dào le.

By plane, you get there in only two hours.

Common mistake: Do not put 就 before the time word; it follows the time phrase.

Practice this point

  1. Say “He finished in ten minutes.” (quickly)

  2. Say “I got up at five.” (very early)

Show model answers and explanations

  1. 他十分钟就做完了。

    就 after the duration signals speed.

  2. 我五点就起床了。

    就 after the time stresses earliness.

G21 · Describe distance with 离

Pattern: A + 离 + B + 远 / 近 / distance

离 measures the distance between two places or between now and an event: 我家离公司很远 “My home is far from the company.” The thing you are describing comes first, then 离 + the reference point, then the distance.

我家离公司很近。

Wǒ jiā lí gōng sī hěn jìn.

My home is close to the company.

离考试还有两天。

Lí kǎo shì hái yǒu liǎng tiān.

There are still two days until the exam.

Common mistake: Do not use 从 for static distance: 我家从学校很远 is wrong; use 离.

Practice this point

  1. Say “The airport is far from here.”

  2. Ask “Is the station near your home?”

Show model answers and explanations

  1. 机场离这儿很远。

    A + 离 + B + adjective.

  2. 火车站离你家近吗？

    近 is the predicate; 吗 makes the question.

G22 · Make a point with sentence-final 呢

Pattern: statement + 呢 (confirming, often exaggerating)

At the end of a statement, 呢 can firmly point out a fact and persuade the listener, often with a hint of “you know!” or exaggeration. It goes after an adjective or verb phrase, often together with 还.

八点上课，时间还早呢。

Bā diǎn shàng kè, shí jiān hái zǎo ne.

Class is at eight; it’s still early, you know.

坐公共汽车要一个多小时呢！

Zuò gōng gòng qì chē yào yí ge duō xiǎo shí ne!

The bus takes over an hour!

Common mistake: Do not confuse this statement 呢 with question 呢 (“what about …?”). This 呢 is not a question.

Practice this point

  2. Say “The hospital is still far away (you know).”

Show model answers and explanations

  1. 还早呢。

    还 + adjective + 呢 makes the point.

  2. 医院离这儿还远呢。

    呢 adds the persuading tone.

G23 · Ask for agreement with ……，好吗？

Adding 好吗 after a suggestion asks the listener to agree: “…, okay?” It is gentle and polite. 可以吗 is similar but asks for permission. Answer with 好 / 好的 or a polite refusal.

Wǒ men míng tiān zài qù, hǎo ma?

你等我一下，好吗？

Nǐ děng wǒ yí xià, hǎo ma?

G24 · Repeat or delay an action with 再

Pattern: subject + 再 + verb (future)

再 means “again” or “then, later” for an action that has not happened yet. Use it for repeating in the future (再说一遍 “say it again”) or postponing (明天再说 “let’s talk tomorrow”). For past repetition, use 又 (HSK 3).

Qǐng zài shuō yí biàn.

Please say it again.

让我想想再告诉你。

Ràng wǒ xiǎng xiang zài gào su nǐ.

Let me think about it and then tell you.

Practice this point

  2. Say “Let’s eat first, then go.”

    再 for a future repeat.

  2. 我们先吃饭，再去。 / 我们吃了饭再去。

    再 introduces the later action.

G25 · Ask or let someone do something with 让

Pattern: A + 让 + B + verb

In a pivotal sentence, the middle person is both the object of the first verb and the subject of the second: 妈妈让我去 “Mom asks me to go.” 让 means “ask, let, make.” 叫 works similarly in speech.

老师让我们明天来。

Lǎo shī ràng wǒ men míng tiān lái.

The teacher asked us to come tomorrow.

让我看看。

Ràng wǒ kàn kan.

Let me have a look.

Common mistake: Do not insert a word for “to” between B and the verb: 让我去, not 让我要去.

Practice this point

  2. Say “Let me introduce myself.”

Show model answers and explanations

  1. 医生让我多休息。

    医生 → 让 → 我 → 休息.

  2. 让我介绍一下。 / 让我介绍一下我自己。

    让 + 我 + verb.

G26 · Soften an action by doubling the verb

Pattern: verb + verb (AA); two-syllable verb: ABAB

Repeating a verb makes an action brief, casual, or tentative, similar to 一下: 看看 “take a look,” 休息休息 “have a rest.” The second syllable is light. Use it for actions you can do briefly, not for states like 喜欢 or 是.

你尝尝这个菜。

Nǐ cháng chang zhè ge cài.

我们休息休息吧。

Wǒ men xiū xi xiū xi ba.

Let’s have a little rest.

Common mistake: Do not double two-syllable verbs as AABB: 休休息息 is wrong. Do not double non-action verbs: 是是, 有有.

  2. Double 介绍 in “Please introduce yourself.”

Show model answers and explanations

  1. 让我想想。

    AA form with a light second syllable.

  2. 请你介绍介绍你自己。

    Two-syllable verbs double as ABAB.

    从 + question word, then verb.

G33 · Compare with 比

G35 · Describe how an action is done with 得

Pattern: verb + 得 + (很) adjective; verb + object + verb + 得 + adjective

A degree complement evaluates an action: 跑得很快 “runs fast,” 说得不好 “doesn’t speak well.” If there is an object, repeat the verb: 他说汉语说得很好. The negative goes after 得.

你穿得太少了。

Nǐ chuān de tài shǎo le.

You’re wearing too little.

她汉语说得非常好。

Tā Hàn yǔ shuō de fēi cháng hǎo.

She speaks Chinese very well.

Common mistake: Do not put the object directly after 得: 他说得汉语很好 is wrong. Do not negate before the verb: 他说得不好, not 他不说得好.

Practice this point

  1. Say “He runs very fast.”

  2. Say “My younger brother doesn’t sing well.”

Show model answers and explanations

  1. 他跑得很快。

    verb + 得 + 很 + adjective.

  2. 我弟弟唱歌唱得不好。 / 我弟弟歌唱得不好。

    Repeat the verb or front the object; 不 goes after 得.

G36 · Say how much more with 比

Pattern: A + 比 + B + adjective + 一点儿 / 多了 / 得多 / amount

To show the size of a difference, put it after the adjective: 一点儿 “a little,” 多了 or 得多 “much,” or an exact amount like 三岁. 还 or 更 can come before the adjective to mean “even more.”

今天比昨天冷多了。

Jīn tiān bǐ zuó tiān lěng duō le.

Today is much colder than yesterday.

这个比那个贵一点儿。

Zhè ge bǐ nà ge guì yì diǎnr.

This one is a bit more expensive than that one.

Common mistake: Do not put 一点儿 before the adjective in a comparison: 比他一点儿高 is wrong; say 比他高一点儿.

Practice this point

  1. Say “My home is much nearer than yours.”

  2. Say “This one is ten yuan more expensive than that one.”

Show model answers and explanations

  1. 我家比你家近多了。 / 我家比你家近得多。

    The degree follows the adjective.

  2. 这个比那个贵十块钱。

    The exact amount follows the adjective.

G37 · Describe a lasting state with 着

Pattern: verb + 着

着 after a verb shows a state that continues as a result of an action: 门开着 “the door is open,” 他穿着红衣服 “he is wearing red clothes.” It describes how things are, rather than an action in progress.

门开着呢。

Mén kāi zhe ne.

The door is open.

她手里拿着一本书。

Tā shǒu li ná zhe yì běn shū.

She is holding a book in her hand.

Common mistake: Do not use 着 with verbs that have no lasting state, such as 去 or 来. Do not confuse it with 在 for actions in progress.

Practice this point

  1. Say “The door is closed.”

  2. Say “He is wearing black clothes.”

Show model answers and explanations

  1. 门关着。 / 门关着呢。

    verb + 着 describes the state.

  2. 他穿着黑色的衣服。 / 他穿着黑衣服。

    着 shows the continuing state of wearing.

G38 · Make a point with 不是……吗

Pattern: 不是 + statement + 吗？

This is a rhetorical question: the speaker believes the statement is true and is reminding or questioning the listener, often with surprise: “Isn’t it …?” The expected answer is yes.

你不是去北京了吗？

Nǐ bú shì qù Běi jīng le ma?

Didn’t you go to Beijing? (I thought you had.)

这不是你的手机吗？

Zhè bú shì nǐ de shǒu jī ma?

Isn’t this your phone?

Common mistake: Do not read this as a negative question asking for information. It expresses what the speaker thinks is true.

Practice this point

  1. Remind a friend that today is their birthday.

  2. What does 你不是喜欢喝咖啡吗？ imply?

Show model answers and explanations

  1. 今天不是你的生日吗？

    不是…吗 implies “it is your birthday.”

  2. The speaker believes the listener likes coffee.

    The rhetorical question asserts the opposite of its surface negative.

G39 · Give a direction with 往

Pattern: 往 + direction / place + verb

往 means “toward.” It goes before a movement verb: 往前走 “walk forward,” 往左拐 “turn left.” It is very common in directions.

一直往前走，就到宾馆了。

Yì zhí wǎng qián zǒu, jiù dào bīn guǎn le.

Go straight ahead and you’ll reach the hotel.

到了路口往右走。

Dào le lù kǒu wǎng yòu zǒu.

At the intersection, go right.

Common mistake: Do not put 往 after the verb: 走往前 is wrong in this pattern.

Practice this point

  1. Say “Walk left.”

  2. Say “Go towards the station.”

Show model answers and explanations

  1. 往左走。

    往 + direction + verb.

  2. 往火车站走。

    往 + place + movement verb.

G40 · Talk about an experience with 过

Pattern: verb + 过 (+ object); negative: 没 + verb + 过

过 after a verb says that someone has had the experience at some point in the past: 我去过中国 “I have been to China.” The negative is 没 (有) + verb + 过: “have never …”. Ask with 吗 or 没有.

你看过那个电影吗？

Nǐ kàn guo nà ge diàn yǐng ma?

Have you seen that movie?

我没吃过羊肉。

Wǒ méi chī guo yáng ròu.

I have never eaten mutton.

Common mistake: Do not use 不 with 过: 我不去过 is wrong. Do not confuse 过 (experience) with 了 (completed action).

Practice this point

  1. Say “I have been to Beijing twice.”

  2. Say “He has never learned Chinese.”

Show model answers and explanations

  1. 我去过两次北京。 / 我去过北京两次。

    过 + frequency.

  2. 他没学过汉语。

    没 + verb + 过.

G41 · Contrast two ideas with 虽然…但是

Pattern: 虽然 + fact，但是 + contrasting result

虽然 means “although” and 但是 means “but.” Chinese normally uses both together, unlike English, which uses only one. 虽然 can go before or after the subject.

虽然今天是晴天，但是很冷。

Suī rán jīn tiān shì qíng tiān, dàn shì hěn lěng.

Although it is sunny today, it is very cold.

他虽然很忙，但是每天都运动。

Tā suī rán hěn máng, dàn shì měi tiān dōu yùn dòng.

Although he is busy, he exercises every day.

Common mistake: Translating literally, learners sometimes drop 但是. In Chinese, keep both words.

Practice this point

  1. Combine: 这件衣服很贵 / 我很喜欢.

  2. Say “Although he is young, he knows a lot.”

Show model answers and explanations

  1. 虽然这件衣服很贵，但是我很喜欢。

    虽然 for the fact, 但是 for the contrast.

  2. 他虽然很小，但是知道很多。 / 虽然他年纪小，但是他知道很多。

    Keep both connectors.

G42 · Count how many times with 次

Pattern: verb + number + 次 (+ object)

次 counts the number of times an action happens. It comes after the verb: 去了两次 “went twice.” With an ordinary object it usually comes before the object; with a pronoun or place name, it may come after.

我看过三次这个电影。

Wǒ kàn guo sān cì zhè ge diàn yǐng.

I have seen this movie three times.

请你再说一次。

Qǐng nǐ zài shuō yí cì.

Please say it one more time.

Common mistake: Do not put the frequency before the verb as in English “twice went.” 两次去 is wrong.

Practice this point

  1. Say “I have read this book twice.”

  2. Ask “How many times have you been to China?”

Show model answers and explanations

  1. 这本书我看过两次。 / 我看过两次这本书。

    The frequency follows the verb (and 过).

  2. 你去过几次中国？ / 你去过中国几次？

    几次 asks about frequency.

G43 · Say something is about to happen with 要…了

Pattern: (快 / 就) + 要 + verb + 了

要…了 means “about to.” 快要…了 means “soon” and 就要…了 often appears with a specific time: 下个月就要回国了. The 了 at the end is required.

新年就要到了。

Xīn nián jiù yào dào le.

The New Year is coming.

快要下雨了，我们回家吧。

Kuài yào xià yǔ le, wǒ men huí jiā ba.

It’s about to rain; let’s go home.

Common mistake: Do not use 快要…了 with a specific time word: 明天快要… is wrong; say 明天就要….

Practice this point

  1. Say “The train is about to leave.”

  2. Say “He is leaving next week.”

Show model answers and explanations

  1. 火车要开了。 / 火车快要开了。

    要…了 for something imminent.

  2. 他下个星期就要走了。

    就要…了 with a specific time.

G44 · Emphasize a reached point with 都…了

Pattern: 都 + time / quantity / state + 了

都…了 means “already” and emphasizes that a point has been reached, often with surprise or impatience: 都十点了 “It’s already ten!” It is stronger and more emotional than 已经.

都十二点了，快睡觉吧。

Dōu shí èr diǎn le, kuài shuì jiào ba.

It’s already twelve o’clock; go to sleep.

他都二十岁了，还不会做饭。

Tā dōu èr shí suì le, hái bú huì zuò fàn.

He’s already twenty and still can’t cook.

Common mistake: Do not confuse this 都 with 都 meaning “all.” Here it signals emphasis on time or degree.

Practice this point

  1. Say “It’s already eight o’clock!”

  2. Say “I’ve waited for an hour already!”

Show model answers and explanations

  1. 都八点了！

    都 + time + 了.

  2. 我都等了一个小时了！

    都 emphasizes the duration.
